# INTRODUCTION

This book is very different from others on the topic of getting tasks done. It is meatier than most. The exercises are unique and guaranteed to work for you and your organization.

Getting a task done is not just a simple matter of sitting down and doing the job. You are not a robot, and the world is not run by artificial intelligence. You have enough experience in life to know that there is much more to it than planting yourself in a chair and going from start to finish. In these pages, there are exercises using not just one, but multiple workable system replete with actions for completing your task in the least amount of time. For the accomplishment of a task, there is a multitude of rewards. One of them is a sense of goal achievement, but the other more important reward, is self-transformation. Incorporated in this handy book are the concepts of the dynamic growth mindset, methods of dealing with the pitfalls of procrastination, and a resetting of attitudes in order to overcome failures. All of the examples in this book are true to life, although names and places have been changed. Getting Sh\*t Done is a reality-based book.

If you apply the principles in this book, you will develop the heart of an athlete and the stubbornness of a mule.

# TRUE GRIT

“The meanest one is Rooster

Cogburn. He is a pitiless

man, double-tough, and

fear doesn’t enter into his

thinking.”

-From the John Wayne

movie ‘True Grit’

‘Grit” is the essential ingredient of Getting Sh\*t Done. It is as important as meat is to a hamburger. Grit beats out muscular power by miles. It is motivation increased exponentially. It is power.

If you were to ask for the exact meaning of “grit,” “They” will present you with all sorts of synonyms for “Grit” –

Courage

Determination

Doggedness

Perseverance

Tenacity

Fortitude

Resolve

Endurance

Persistence

Bravery

Commitment

Just who are “They” who are defining that term? “They” are the folks you assume are more successful, more accomplished, better, smarter, faster, and more efficient than you are. Those are the people from the tribe of “They.” Those are the ones who get more tasks done in an hour than most others. Those are the people who earn more money, get more promotions, and reap the rewards of success. Those folks have “True Grit,” and you want to join the tribe of “They” if you can.

Angela Duckworth, professor of psychology at the University of Pennsylvania, has developed the concept of “grit.” She defines it as:

1.  The tendency not to abandon tasks for mere changeability. Not seeking something because of novelty. Not ‘looking for a change.’
2.  The tendency not to abandon tasks in the face of obstacles. Perseverance, tenacity, doggedness.

Grit is the life blood of motivation. It is the sheer courage of an athlete. It is the substance of a hero. It is there for the taking.

Perhaps “grit” is something you feel you don’t have and can never achieve. Believe it or not, but you are hard-wired to have grit. Surely you have seen a child stand up and take his or her first steps. First the child shakes and wobbles, trying desperately to get those bowlegs to work in service of the body they carry. After the first fall, the child does it again…and again. The child may postpone the effort to let his or her battered bottom recover, but will try…and try again. You have never seen a two-year old who doesn’t walk. Grit is hard-wired into your human body and mind.

In the 18th century in America, the settlers went west. When they reached the Rocky Mountains, they climbed one mountain, and then they climbed the next one, and the next one until they reached fertile soils of California. They had true grit.

THE GROWTH MINDSET\
FOR GETTING TASKS \
DONE
===================

“Today there is much more

universal interest in…a

lifestyle practice necessary

to deal with the new world

most all of us are

experiencing.”

-David Allen

The quotation cited above elucidates Allen’s approach to task-focused behavior. In his work, Allen addresses the hefty topic of Getting Things Done. A primary feature of his GETTING THINGS DONE (GTD) system is “lifestyle practice.” It is equivalent to the “Growth Mindset” dynamic introduced by Carol Dweck. It represents the kind of attitude that yields concrete results.

In the beginning, a child takes in a tremendous amount of stimulation, but ever so quickly filters out all that input.  Then – before the parents realize it – the child is ready to start school. Every parent around the world is aghast at the speed at which the child developed the ability to sort out his mental data. Well, that child is still within you. It can and will adapt and grow rapidly. As a child, you learned how to rifle through all the clutter your senses visited upon you. The child within you placed sensory items into your long-term memory, and retained only the ones you immediately need. When that occurred, you were anxious to take on any full-scale task. Children around the world will tell you they want to have their schooling done as quickly as possible…and even quicker. They would love to have that task – done in one sitting! Children want to grow, not only physically, but mentally. Why? Because they want to be able to do anything you can do (and do it even better than you). Children have what is called the GROWTH MINDSET. According to Carol Dweck, the growth mindset is the belief that your initial aptitudes and temperaments can be tailored to change and grow in order to meet the tremendous challenges life presents.

The term “Mindset” has evolved since its first use in 1909. In some contexts, it was considered to be a fixed set of beliefs and behaviors of a group of people, a culture, and other groups that they found effective –  the proverbial “status quo.” Even the Merriam-Webster Dictionary defines it as a “fixed state of mind.” Today, that is no longer the prevailing belief. In fact, in 1980, Maruyama, who called it “mindscape,” indicated that the concept is richly varied. Subtly, he introduced the notion that there can be two different types of “mindscapes” when he said: “Nature should be kept unchanged” vs “Nature is changing all the time.”

In her monumental studies, Carol Dweck from Stanford University delineated two types of mindsets: 1) the fixed mindset, and 2) the growth mindset as discussed above.

Those of you who function with the growth mindset, are not saddled with the stigma of a “fixed” IQ. The folks operating within the fixed mindset believe that their success is based upon IQ. What’s more, they feel that they are limited by that constraint.

Because of today’s rapid pace, you need to go with the flow, like a boat plying the rapids. The technique of steering and moving forward gets you to the end of your journey. You cannot plant your oars in the water and expect to hold the boat in one place like a rock. Rocks resist. Boats go with the flow because they have to. The “fixed mindset” is passé. It doesn’t work in today’s world unless you are a hermit.

If you want to get a task done, then you want to proceed with doing it in a relaxed and focused way. Otherwise, all of your energy will be expended in erratic bursts of frenetic activity. Everyone has had the experience of walking into one of those “Big Box” stores and being bombarded with signs, displays, items of all sizes and shapes, shopping carts, noises, and colors everywhere the eye can see. On the TV and the Internet, you are likewise assaulted with every distraction imaginable. That pommeling is a deliberate sabotage designed by clever marketers to lead you into making it your task to buy something from them. If you want to get a task done, it is you who must take charge, not them. It is you, and not they, who will accomplish your tasks. It is you who wants to perform a task at one sitting, not them. The marketers are rooted in the “FIXED MINDSET” of getting more and more customers to purchase their products over and over again.  Have you ever been to a trade show or the like and heard someone behind a table saying the same thing to every new passer-by? Over and over again ad nauseam. B-O-O-R-I-N-G! No wonder every computer app has a Copy and Paste function.

Like the child who was bombarded with sensations, you have an excruciating desire to delete the distractions and corral the three elements you need to do your task:

1.  Space
2.  Tools
3.  Time

Space

This is not so much a physical space as it is a psychological space. The mind has many labyrinthine corridors leading to many rooms, but you want to allocate only those that you need to get the job done. You want no emotional or mental interruptions.

Tools

These are the physical and/or the intellectual apparatuses needed to complete the task. All of the necessary tools need to be within your immediate grasp. All of the unnecessary tools need to placed aside. On your computer, you want the unused apps closed up and set aside for use at another time.

Time

This book classifies “Time” as a sense. Everyone has a “sense of time,” after all. Time is illusory and transient. It is intangible, but has a conceptual reality of its own. Time is the most important facet of your task, because you want to get your task done at one sitting. When you make an error, can you go back in time and reclaim the time you spent? No. Kurt Kruse has said “You can never lose time and get it back again.” Instead, you have to do that segment over again, thus consuming more precious time. You want to get your task done at one sitting, not two or three.

FIND OUT YOUR PRIME TIME

Everyone has unique daily cycles. Your “Prime Time” is that segment of the day during which your brain is at its optimum level of functioning. This is the time during the day during which you rarely glance at the time. Think about it, and ask yourself: During what times do I tend to ignore the time? That is the natural segment of your day when you steam ahead at full throttle. Designate your prime time for the one major task or tasks you want to get done. Decide that you can and you will get it done during your prime time.

“Sue’s Glue”

If you have ever wandered in the open-air offices of large corporations, you will see dozens of desks and chairs. What you will see very, very often are deserted work stations. Where is everyone? If the volume was turned way up in the building, you would hear the thunder of steps from 9 to 5. Everyone is walking about. People are at the vending machines, in the bathrooms, in the cafeteria, leaning against the walls of a hallway chattering with each other, at their lockers, or hanging out in empty conference rooms. They are not “at their desks.” They are non-productive. They are not getting the job done.

“Sue’s Glue”
An enterprising mother by the name of Sue often took the children to a restaurant to give them the experience of learning how to behave at the table in public. Before they left the house, Sue instructed her children to imagine that their little bottoms were glued to their chairs. They were permitted to move around naturally, as long as their bottoms stayed ‘glued’ to their chairs. Of course, all children have a lot of energy, so Sue’s children did look around, pick up and replace their silverware, draw on the placemats designed for children, turn around, bend down, and the like. All the time, their bottoms were riveted to their seats. Sometimes the relatives joined the family at these restaurants. Everyone marveled at the self-control the children displayed, especially when the group paused between courses.  

Of course you are not a child, but the same self-discipline is essential throughout life. Once you’re an adult, you have many more tasks to do, and need to develop the discipline to sort out your tasks in meaningful segments before you tackle them. Of course you don’t want to do that. It is like sorting laundry. Icccck! Human nature tells you to rush about doing tasks haphazardly. Why? Because it is more fun that way!

As ridiculous as it sounds, imagine that your bottom is “glued” to the seat of your chair, and you are committed to staying in that one place until the task is done. Some people use desks that elevate to allow themselves to benefit from more conducive blood flow. However, your self-imposed confinement is there – “glued” at your desk.

Occasionally, it is important that you walk around your work space (not the building) in order to keep your blood flowing vigorously. Make those excursions around your desk short and sweet. Think about your project during your walks. Many people can garner new ideas while walking. In fact, you may happily discover that you are driven to return to your desk to write down your fresh inspirations. Your creative muses like being heeded.

DON’T GET CRAZY!

Thomas Edison, inventor of the light bulb, phonograph, and movie-related optical devices, had his famous desk in West Orange, NJ, USA. The desk is still there to this day. However, his cot is there too. That is NOT the kind of life you need. Edison didn’t have the advantage of computers. He had to use the “trial-and-error” method which was all manual so it did take him longer than it would today. Yes, there are some people who are so obsessed with their work, that it becomes their life.

Actually, Edison did spend time in his West Orange home, but worked long and hard hours. That may not in your best interest. Marriages can disintegrate. Learn to delegate if matters get out of hand. You, your family, and your co-workers can alleviate the burden. 

A Task Example

Let’s say you are in charge of preparing for an all-day conference. You want to plan that at one sitting. At the beginning of your day, or the day before, take the three elements of your task:

- Time

Because it is the most vital element, allot a certain amount of time to the complete the accomplishment of organizing the task. Examining the scope of that task, you might allot three or four hours to do that.

- Space

You now need the physical space for doing it, which would be your desk and chair. You also need the psychological space. That entails placing aside all the unrelated things in your mind. You are going to pick up your suit at the cleaners later. That gets filed into your “Later” tray.

The process of designating psychological space also involves elimination of distractions so you should consider turning off your cell phone, closing your office door, and the like. You are in charge; the distractions have no status.

- Tools

Your tools are those apps on your computer required to do the task. They might mercifully include a coffee maker and a package of lozenges.  Be sure you have ALL the tools you need together in one place.

Do not dread pausing long enough to think at the onset of the task. In your mind, create a logical system for performing the task.

For example, in preparing a conference, you’re going to first make a rough list of the topics that needs to be covered in order to prepare for the conference. They may not be in order, but they will be there. Type them out on your computer in the tool you have selected. Perhaps it is Microsoft Word®.  Reflect to determine if anything is missing and add it in – anywhere. Reflect to see if you can add a little creativity to your wording. Add some unexpected and “fun” words. Your conference needs to draw the attention of all its participants. Secondly, divide each topic into sub-topics. \*Do not stop now…you are on a roll! \*

Thirdly, reflect on the list carefully. Decide if you want to rearrange any of the topics. Decide if all of your sub-topics are in the proper sequence and occupy the right place. Make the appropriate changes. As new topics and sub-topics come to mind, add them.

Fourthly, organize all the material into a presentable chronological package keeping in mind the “cosmetics” of the situation. A visual look at your reorganized document should bring you some degree of pleasure. That is a great incentive for continuing the task.

Next you would need to flesh out each sub-topic with a few sentences or keywords.

Lastly, you need to assign certain people to carry the responsibility for the various segments you have named. It will be their job to brainstorm the topics and sub-topics with the goal of making the conference functional, instructive, and practical.

Flexibility is essential here. If a member of your team comes up with a fresh approach to one of your topics, by all means consider it. However, the conference preparation is your task, so you need to maintain some level of responsibility and control. There is no room for wild mustangs, although you can appreciate their ingenuity.

MIND WANDERING \
& THE ‘TO DO’ LIST
==================

“To be everywhere is

to be nowhere.”

-Seneca

Everyone has a ‘To Do’ list. Besides the pedestrian duties on people’s ‘To Do’ list, people maintain more than one major goal. Bluma Zeigarnik discovered that incomplete goals served as a distraction from the current immediate goal at hand. That is called the “Zeigarnik effect.” In their study, E.J. Masicampo and Roy F. Baumeister of Florida State University concluded that “Once a detailed plan has been made, one no longer has to think about the goal to execute it…Thoughts of an incomplete goal will not interfere with current concerns so long as a plan has been made to see the goal through later on.”

Most folks have multiple pursuits. No one can focus upon all of them at the same time. “Multitasking” is generally a fallacy, but no one has a one-track mind. So, how does one prevent being distracted from a current task by other incomplete items on the infamous ‘To Do’ list?

To simply relegate organization of your incomplete goals to your memory is a poor use of memory. It is not the function of your memory to organize and strategize stepwise solutions for incomplete goals. Otherwise, your mind will wander throughout the day while you are trying to get things done. That creates cognitive tension and is the condition of a strained system.

Your ever-vigilant mind will want to focus on organizing an incomplete goal via the various sub-tasks needed to complete the goal. That will distract you from the task at hand. So, what do you do? You outline a plan for accomplishment of the incomplete goals residing in your mind. Then, and only then, can you file them away in the “Later” tray in your memory.

GOAL PLANNING FOR THE ‘DO-LATER’ TASKS

You need to create a general plan for the completion of goals to take place at a future time. Suppose that planning for an all-day conference is on your agenda for the day. Also suppose that you want to write a novel in the near future. That is another goal. According to Jack Echo, it is imperative that you “Capture all the things that might need to get done or have usefulness for you – now, later, someday, big, little, or in between – in a logical and trusted system outside your head and off your mind.” Research by Baumeister and other cognitive and social psychologists indicates that you have a “quasi-need” to develop a general plan for accomplishing your secondary goal(s). That will put your mind at rest so you can concentrate on your daily agenda. It is best to write that down or record it digitally. That way, your memory will engage in the actions for which it was intended.

Corralling Your Daily Agenda

In the old cartoons, you have often seen an employee’s desk with two

trays on it. One is labeled “IN” and the other is labeled “OUT.” Your breakneck objective for each day is to get all the items lying in your “IN” box into the “OUT” box.

Each item in your “IN” box craves specificity. A note such as “Meet with Denise” is too general. A rewording such as “Email Denise to schedule an appointment” works far better because it is actionable.

If a few items in the “IN” box can be done in a matter of minutes, do them now. Once you have the pesky tasks done, your mind is less stressed out.

If there are items in your “IN” box that will take more time, try delegating them to someone else or file them into: a) your trash, b) your “Someday/maybe” tray, or c) your reference tray for later retrieval. Write or record items in the b and c tray. They must not be in your “IN” box. Now, you are ready to tackle your major task(s) of the day during your “prime time.”

The road leading from the “IN” box to your “OUT” box is a one-way street. That is a cardinal rule.

In their studies, the CIA points out that actionable intelligence is primarily a cognitive function. Later, it may require psychomotor skills. The actions are specific enough that they can be acted upon without further analysis. Those kinds of tasks are to be relegated to your “Prime Time” functioning. Your priority tasks belong in that time segment.

Once you have allotted three or four hours of your “Prime Time” to a major task, pause to reflect upon it. This is not a time-wasting daydream. It is a cognitive exercise in organization.

Permit yourself to “obsess” on that priority task to the exclusion of all others. Use a dose of “Sue’s Glue” to make that happen.

Perhaps you have never considered the organizational planning of one task. Below is an excellent formula that is applicable to all tasks. Copy it and save it as a framework for any task you must complete.

What are the expected outcome(s) and purpose(s) of your task?
What are the various things you need to meet the requirements of time, space, and tools?
What is the first action required in order to launch your task?
What are the follow-up actions required to keep the project rolling? List them.

Your actions probably won’t be in chronological order. It is necessary for you to submit to the creative
bombardment of ideas and inspirations. Those do not
arrive in chronological order. Your imagination is like
an open box. Ideas arrive in a haphazard fashion. It is 
the function of your cognitive brain to sort them out. Therefore:

Organize the overall task into chronological segments.
Review the actions you have listed to ascertain if there are any ones that are missing, if items need to be rearranged, or if items need to be abandoned. 
Design a tracking system to follow-up with the actions you have set in motion. For example, one of your actions might be to email company supervisors regarding your plan. Because the supervisors will not answer the email immediately, you need to follow-up with them to confirm they have read your email and have initiated their roles in moving the task along.
Record the actions you have planned or write them down.

OK. Now the project is done. That has now moved to your “OUT” tray. Congratulations! The task is complete…or is it?

Stumbling blocks will arise even before you have completed your task. You will have to modify your plan accordingly. That is why you needed to allow for some flexibility in your schedule. You planned for the task to take three to four hours. That is a comfortable range.

There will be necessary interruptions, despite your heroic efforts to keep them extinct. When you deal with computers, there will be glitches and incompatibilities that you must handle. You may have to save your incomplete major task, place it aside, and gallop all over the Internet looking for corrective or replacement software. It is maddening, but that is why you planned a time range within your “Prime Time” schedule.

There is an old adage: “Music calms the savage beast.” It is a wise saying because background music does have a calming effect on the human nervous system. Try using it to foster your patience.

There are two types of thinking people may employ to tackle a task. It depends upon their personal thinking style. Both styles are legitimate, but only one style can be used at a time to approach a task:

1.  Linear Thinking \
    This thinking style tends to follow a stepwise progressive path from a main topic or theme. A linear thinking model is like a tree. There is one main trunk, and all else stems from that trunk.
2.  Abstract Thinking \
    This thinking style tends to follow a more functional path. An abstract thinking model is like a house on a piece of property. A tree will fall if you cut through the trunk. A house will not fall down if you damage one corner. An abstract thinker will design a house with rooms based upon functions, and the yard will also be designed that way. For example, there will be traditional rooms in the house, non-traditional rooms, plus a patio, a deck, a hot tub room, a playhouse and play equipment in the backyard.

Your thinking style might be linear. If you are presented with a general goal or purpose for which you have to develop a plan, you will most likely design a more structured stepwise plan that follows a vertical path. For example, “First I do this, then I do that, then I follow-up with this, etc.”

Exercise Example A in this chapter is designed linearly. It is interactive, but adheres to one pattern. Exercise Example B is designed more abstractly. It is also interactive, but in a different way.

If your thinking style is more abstract, you will tend to think in terms of the trite expression: “Form follows function.” Your plan will be interactive but might run on multiple tracks. For example, you might hold brainstorming sessions, then break off participants into groups like: “role-playing,” “expressive practices like public speaking,” “exercises in communication,” “discussion groups,” “games groups,” etc.

\*A Wise Word about Task Management Tools

Some people benefit from management software apps designed to help organize a task in an efficient manner, e.g., within the “Prime Time” of your working day. However, marketers are afoot. If you tend to be an abstract thinker, your technique of handling the task of promoting better employee teamwork cooperation may very well be different from that of those who prefer to use a linear thinking model.

# MEETINGS AND COMMITTEES

“A camel is a horse designed

by a committee.”

-Anon.

During the course of following the task management systems proposed in this book, it is sometimes necessary that you set up brainstorming sessions and the like for input. This is the input you may need to design a desired outcome. Meetings are the least favorite activity of workers, but can be a necessary tool for getting the tasks done.

Task efficiency depends upon time management. When you are assigned a task, especially one that is new to you, it may be helpful to get the input of others so as not to repeat mistakes. At the onset of a task, there are some people who will make a gazillion phone calls asking someone else how they should go about doing it. That alone can fill up three or more hours of your precious time. In addition, confusion arises because you will be hit with many different opinions, some of which may be contradictory. Consulting a lot of people will only delay your task.

HOW NOT TO SET YOURSELF UP FOR FAILURE

Do not secure many opinions from co-workers, family, and friends. Because people often they must point out weaknesses rather than strengths, you might end up being totally discouraged before you embark on a project. That is the “private committee.”

Also, try not to get timid about negative feedback. There are a lot of grouchy and jealous people out there. Only when too many are saying the same thing, should you revise your project planning.

MEETINGS ARE NEVER FUN

Nobody likes meetings. Of course, meetings and committees are essential, but they need to be kept to a minimum. Also, the input of information must be kept to a minimum. If you are trying to sell a product, a meeting does not do the actual selling. Meetings and committees exist for the purposes of:

- Company cohesion
- Goal planning
- Monitoring progress
- Brainstorming
- News

Tip: Never schedule a meeting very early in the morning.

Believe it or not, many people – including executives – break those cardinal rules. If a meeting is scheduled too early in the morning, many employees simply are not in the mood to approach the task with an open mind. Let’s face it. Most need a boost from morning coffee or tea. People have had to go through the annoyance of commuting to work. They need a brief recovery stage during which they can “change channels.”

When you are presented with a task, sometimes it is necessary that you elicit input. That entails calling for a brainstorming session or a meeting. Here are the most efficient steps you need to take in order to do this as quickly and as painlessly as possible:

1.  Delineate an agenda.
2.  Impose time schedules for discussion of the items on the agenda.
3.  Stay focused on the main purpose of the meeting. In practice, that means glancing back at the main purpose while the group is engaged in the discussion.
4.  Lead the members back to the main topic if they wander too far astray.
5.  Recruit the smallest number of key people needed to participate in the meeting.
6.  Keep audio-visual presentations short and to the point.
7.  Keep reminding yourself and the others of the final objective of the meeting and the decision(s) you want to reach at the end of the meeting.
8.  Take notes.
9.  Summarize the points made by the participants and ask for quick feedback on those points alone. No ancillary data.
10. Review the decisions(s) reached by the group.

Tip: Never repeat something for a latecomer. He or she will have to get the gist of it from the succeeding content.

Tip: Keep an open mind. The purpose of your meeting is to be informative, but also to secure the input of others. Office politics should not be allowed to enter into the meeting room.

The Chairperson

You are the chairperson of the meeting. While maintaining the proper etiquette, be sure you do not lose control of the meeting. Do your best to keep people from interrupting each other. Very often you have seen that in news commentaries. When the leader isn’t dominant enough, the show descends into chaos. When the leader is too dominant, it is a turn-off.

Your objective for a meeting is to get a consensus and reach a decision as how to proceed with your task. Remind committee members of that if necessary.

The Meeting Members

Select the right people to participate in your meeting. Those people may not be friends of yours necessarily. Also, they do not need to be managers or executives. After all, this is your task. You hardly want your manager to attend because you will feel that you must follow

whatever their advice is, even though it might not help accomplish

the task at hand. Trust yourself and give yourself the credit of

knowing that you can deal with the task.

There was once a large manufacturing plant that was broken up in different divisions. The first division used an apparatus to move machinery that they had purchased from a vendor. However, once the machinery they had was heavier due to added improvements, the chains on the apparatus broke. They they had to purchase a different apparatus.

The second division of the same pant asked if they could have the old apparatus for moving their equipment. They knew that the chains tended to break, but did not have the money to buy a more expensive unit. So they tasked themselves with the objective of resolving the chain problem. When they received the old device, they held a brainstorming meeting. Members were few, but they were selected based on their knowledge and innovative skills. No managers were present. Then the chairperson presented the issue. Very shortly into the meeting, a guy at the end raised his hand. Now, this fellow was just a man on the assembly line. When he was recognized by the chairperson, the man said only two words: “Use titanium.”

So the plant had a titanium chain developed and it worked fine. That was the shortest committee meeting in the history of the plant.

Never underestimate someone based on their status. Even when you are developing a tenable system for use in your family, do not underestimate your children. They can often come up with unique solutions for household problems. Successful task-doers employ all available resources around them. That is called “Thinking Outside the Box.”

# PROCRASTINATION Is blasphemous

Exercise: Try saying the name of this chapter three or four times. It’s a tongue twister. One of the examples in this chapter cites the use of tongue twisters as a means of conquering a challenging task you may have in the future (public speaking).

“Procrastination is

one of the most

common and deadliest

of diseases and its

toll on success and

happiness is heavy.”

-Wayne Gretsky

Joseph Ferrari, from the American Psychological Association states: “We all put tasks off, but my research has found that 20 percent of U.S. men and women are chronic procrastinators…that’s higher than the number of people diagnosed with clinical depression or phobias.”

People procrastinate for a variety of reasons – most of which are self-defeating. This happens at work; it happens in life. The reasons for procrastination revolve around a very similar set of irrational beliefs. That belief system is not only cognitive, but emotive. Here is a sample of a set of typical irrational beliefs that prevent one from completing a task:

1.  I am incompetent.
2.  I cannot learn new things.
3.  No one likes me.
4.  I am stupid.
5.  I am inexperienced.
6.  I cannot handle criticism.
7.  I know my limitations.
8.  People do not trust me.
9.  That lies outside my comfort zone.
10. I am depressed.
11. I feel sick.
12. It is not fun to do those tasks.
13. The task is too burdensome.
14. That task is a waste of my time.
15. Others can do it much faster than I can.
16. I can get by without doing that tiresome task.
17. “That’s above my pay grade.”

Those beliefs are commingled with feelings of fear. Once that irrational belief system is triggered, it sets off a real bodily response. The hormone adrenaline gushes into the bloodstream due to one or more of those thoughts, and a person is faced with the fight – flight behavioral response. When something is perceived to be a threat, procrastinators feel afraid and want to run away. The task at work is a stressor and raises one’s anxiety level, but it is “good stress.”

Anxiety is normal. What makes it abnormal is the degree to which it is experienced. However, sometimes exceptions must be made to suit the circumstances. For example, you are asked to make a speech or presentation to some business executives. Let’s say that you have never given a speech like that in public before. Many of those irrational beliefs above will immediately rise to the surface and cause you more anxiety than that guy in the marketing department who makes presentations all the time. For you, the higher degree of anxiety you feel is normal considering your circumstances, i.e., the newness of it.

Avoidance of New Tasks

Real abnormality sets in when you adopt the attitude that you are incapable of the task. The task at hand has now been converted from a challenge into a personal affront. How dare they ask me to make a fool of myself! Next, that person changes the irrational belief into an irrational decision. That is pure and unadulterated acceptance of defeat before the battle is fought.

Parents often tell their children that they must do things they do not feel like doing. The little boy walks into band practice holding a trumpet he has never blown into before. Unlike children, adults are less willing to try new things. Yet, it is just the same experience. As long as you are human, you are capable of learning. People in their 70’s and 80’s are very capable of using computers. And they do! How? Because they tried it the first time. Then they tried it the second time, the third time, and so on. A house is built brick by brick, not all at once.

Avoidance of Disliked Tasks

Task avoidance stems from an unwillingness to produce an outcome. People in this category have lost interest in the value of the outcome. It is hard thing to admit that one may be selfish. Because the outcome is not valuable to the doer does not make it a worthless task. For example, some people complain about checking items off a list in a database to satisfy their clients because those items have little value to the doer. They consider it a waste of time and money. However, the information is valuable to their clients. Therefore, it is necessary – a necessary ‘evil’ if you will, but necessary for job survival. Job survival is the value that applies to the doers of the task. They just overlooked that fact.

Other tasks are simply unpleasant or perhaps perceived as potentially embarrassing. A person has been assigned to make a presentation, but feels they lack a good speaking voice. Then it is a case of taking on the added task of learning good diction. There are techniques for that, including practice with tongue twisters. Such methods can turn a disliked or dreaded task into fun.

Delay of Gratification

Everybody wants instant satisfaction. However, that is not possible in the real world. Nevertheless, some people wander around procrastinating while entertaining the fantasy that success will visit them spontaneously. Of course, that’s insane. Work is just that – work. It is not always fun. Gratification and pleasure visits when the task is done. That kind of gratification is far, far greater than any gratification that might come from eating your favorite candy bar. The gratification felt in getting a major (or even minor task done) is one of self-fulfillment. Candy does not meet that standard by any means. Biochemically, success at a task – particularly a difficult one – releases a neurotransmitter\* called dopamine. Dopamine is utilized, in this case, to bring about a sensation of pleasure. Who wants to skip a nice shot of dopamine once in a while?

The Hypochondriac: Excuse Expert Extraordinaire

Sometimes people use the excuse that they feel ill or depressed when they are faced with tasks that must get done. All too often, the anticipation of doing the task actually does cause physical discomfort. Usually that pain in felt in the stomach area. That area houses what is called the celiac plexus. It is a massive collection of nerves in the area of the abdomen. Once those nerves are overstimulated, real pain is felt. The cause is not due to a physical disorder. It is psychological. It is also the most vulnerable area of the body that is affected by stress.

Some people actually experience a let-up of physical symptoms once they realize and admit to themselves that the cause of the stress is psychological. Some use meditation. Others sometimes need to rely on mild tranquillizers to help them retrain their bodies to react differently to the cognitive stimuli that sets them off.

PANIC: THE PENALTY FOR PROCRASTINATION

If you put off your work task, the time of reckoning lurks ahead. That is an especially vulnerable area. First of all, if you rush through the task at hand, it may not be well done. Secondly, the rush of adrenaline in your system will cloud your brain. It will also affect your body and your sense of self-esteem. The price of procrastination is exorbitant, unnecessary, and sometimes disastrous.

If procrastination becomes a way of life, a person can become immune to panic. He or she may reach the pitiful stage of I don’t care anymore. Disasters go unrecognized. For example, if people put off paying their taxes for too long, they stand to lose everything. There are helpers available to dig them out, if only they did not permit the attitude of “I don’t care” to gain control of their lives.

Sometimes the small tasks can seem chaotic because all of them are different. The small tasks may or may not have significance, or have varying degrees of significance. This makes it necessary to prioritize your smaller work tasks. And – let’s face it – some tasks are not worth doing. # DEALING WITH FAILURE

    “We are flawed.”

\- Tom Cheney

Let’s say you fail at your first attempt to get your major task. You feel bad. That’s OK. Yes, it’s OK to feel bad sometimes. Accomplishment of the task was important to you. It had a lot to do with your feelings of self-esteem. Many people glibly say: “Don’t give up.” It’s easier to say that than to do it, isn’t it? Other people say that it isn’t a disaster to fail at a task. No, it’s not, but it feels like a disaster, doesn’t it? Guess what…it is OK to feel like it’s a disaster, too. But it’s not the end. You are not dead. You only feel bad because accomplishment of the task had far more meaning than the task itself. It meant something about you. You are important and your sense of accomplishment and self-esteem is worth fighting for. So – fight!

If you drive down a road and find out that it is a dead end, what do you do? Everyone turns around and looks for an alternate route. Literally no one turns around and goes home. That attitude of seeking another route defeats failure. You see, there are many roads to success. There is more than one way to get a task done. It is not a question of quitting or not. The issue revolves around:

- modifying your original approach, or
- taking a brand-new approach

Perhaps you used a linear style to outline your task. Maybe what’s needed is a more abstract approach.

THE GOAL

Focus on your goal, not your stumbling. Never lose sight of the prize – self-esteem. The task you had at hand was only one means to achieve self-esteem. Accomplishment of that goal is a war worth fighting.

Perseverance has its own private and personal reward. If you try again using a new strategy, you are persistent. Persistence and perseverance says something about you and will raise your sense of self-esteem. People will look at you and say: “He is persistent. He is to be admired. He will get the job done.” Margaret Thatcher, a former prime minister of Great Britain, once said to the elder George Bush: “Remember, George, this is no time to go wobbly.” The same thing goes for you. Focus on your goal, not the stumbling, and not the hurt you feel. That’s perfectly natural. It’s not a crime to feel hurt, but it should not be your central focus.

The word “failure” should be abolished from the English language. The more proper word is “failures.” Failure is never a permanent experience unless you let it be. Failures are learning experiences. Perhaps there is a weak element in your work or home strategy. Perhaps there are oversights.

REPETITION COMPULSION

Recently, a proctor was assigned the job of mentoring an exam via computer. The proctor was supposed to do it visually via camera and a microphone. As part of the procedure, he sent the student an application that was supposed to trigger a joint interface on the Internet. That didn’t work because the entire application did not download on to the student’s computer. So, the proctor sent it over again to the student. The result was exactly the same. After much frustration on the part of the proctor and student, the proctor asked that another proctor step in. The next proctor did exactly the same thing with exactly the same result. The next proctor did it again…and again… until the student got worn out and postponed the exam. Both were emotionally exhausted.

Why is it that people sometimes do the same thing over and over again when they know it won’t work? The psychologists call that phenomenon “repetition compulsion.” Many people engage in it. That behavior, in actuality, is a form of laziness. It means that a person does not want to stop the behavior and take the extra time to reflect and analyze until he or she can develop an alternate solution. Failure then wins total control. In the above situation, both the student and the proctors felt disappointed.

On the next day, another proctor came by, and tried the same thing. As could be predicted, it had the same result. Then the new proctor asked a few questions. Next, he asked for a brief hiatus of a few minutes. When he returned, he asked the student to use a different web browser, download and install a Flash program, and then he sent the application successfully. The task took more thought and more planning. It needed an alternative solution. Just that and nothing more. The failure did not become a total one because the new proctor was solution-oriented, not “failure-beaten.”

Even billionaires like Warren Buffett readily admit having made blunders – even big ones. Buffett didn’t fold up his tent and quit. Besides, life has a nibby way of continuing.  

Failures happen. How you handle it makes all the difference. Low self-esteem is often the cause. Low self-esteem does not have to be permanent. It can be corrected, but only by those of you who dig down deep within yourself and find the child within. That is the child who fell many times before walking.

It takes sheer and unadulterated courage to try again, but there is only one way out. As in the example of the proctor above, you may need to reflect upon reasons why a strategy did not work. There will always be a reason.

Research the approach you took. Use the Internet, books, and other people who may offer suggestions. The answer is there, somewhere.

Recall, also, that you are probably fostering some irrational beliefs like “I knew I couldn’t do it.” Edison would never have invented the light bulb had he given up without trying it out using a bamboo filament. While Edison’s experiments were in process, Walter Mallory, his associate, commented to Edison that it was such a shame he had worked so hard and there were no results. To that, Edison is quoted as having said: “Results? Why, man, I have gotten lots of results! I know several thousand things that won’t work!” In a funny sense, that is knowledge, and it is a new beginning.

Power: The Real Reward of Conquering the Little Failures

It is true that you will feel good when you accomplish a task successfully. However, it doesn’t stop there. You will want to complete another task…and then another…and yet another. You will not be able to stop. Your dynamic growth mindset will be set into motion. Your self-esteem will increase, but it will become just a side effect. You will lose interest even in that. The esteem of others is also a side effect. What you will achieve is a sense of overall personal satisfaction. That is beyond mere emotion. It is power.

“One of the secrets of a

successful life is to be

able to hold all of our

energies upon one point,

to focus all of the scattered

rays of the mind upon

one place or thing.”

-Dr. Orison S. Marden

Imagine a giant machine in a manufacturing plant. As you listen to it, you can hear a distinctive rhythm. All the complex parts you see are operating in total harmony. There are no chaotic noises. At the far end of the machine, you can see workers quickly picking up fully manufactured products and handing it off to the next person to package. More products come out, then more, and more in rapid succession. If that machine was conscious, it would be concentrating on one and only one thing – the final product.

That kind of focus and attention will accelerate the accomplishment of a task in an efficient amount of time. Let’s say you are in your work cubicle with a large task in front of you. At the end of three or four hours, you bring the completed task to your boss. The kind of focus you must employ is unidirectional. 

In 1988, Crick and Koch performed a study that shook up the field of cognitive studies. Since then, researchers have uncovered the role of gamma brain waves in attention, particularly visual stimulation. This could be physical visualization or mental visualization. This gamma wave state increases insight and perception to a nearly superhuman degree.  When an individual’s awareness reaches that level, the person is able to focus upon one goal, one issue, and one theme to the absolute exclusion of all others. During that time of heightened concentration and consciousness, brain waves in different regions are functioning in complete synchrony.

Gamma brain waves move at a rate of 25 – 100 Hz. This occurs at the ‘breakthrough’ that devoted athletes feel. It is a completely natural “high.” This extremely rapid gamma brain wave movement has been observed in Tibetan monks as well. It can occur during a wakeful and alert stage.  It is a time when a person is engaged in high-level information processing, extreme sports, a total experience of sensations, and mystical experiences. The eyes need not be closed, because physical sight isn’t necessary and doesn’t interfere. Both the body and the mind are functioning at optimal level. It is the “Peak Experience” described by the psychologist, Abraham Maslow. It is an experience of the totality of the dynamic of life itself at all levels. It is the “music of the universe.”

Not only accomplished athletes and philosophers experience this. Prolific creative writers experience it. In fact, many have complained that the thoughts and words gush in faster than they can dictate them or type them. Some writers, like the late Stephen Cannell, has even said that he was mentally writing more than one book in his head simultaneously.

Fortunately for writers, the ability to increase time periods of gamma brain wave patterns enhances memory. Therefore, many of those who cannot type the information fast enough will remember the scenarios and can record them later.

Physiologists and psychologists who are engaged in brain studies note a heightened degree of gamma wave patterns during some periods of dreaming. Although it is theoretical, it is proposed that dreams sometimes take place at the time when the brain is processing information acquired during the day, sorting it out, and storing the wanted information in the halls of long-term memory. The chaos experienced in dreaming is like that of human traffic on a city sidewalk. At that time of day, many different people with many different names, and many different talents and skills are jammed together. Eventually, each of them finds the place where they belong. That is the sorting time.

3.  Select a video on YouTube. Turn the sound off. Attempt to focus on just one imaginary box on the screen. It is more difficult to do with videos. You can try it with TV also, particularly during commercials during which the background pictures change very frequently. If you have never noticed that, take a look at one or two.

Interesting observation: It you only focus on the middle three boxes of a TV screen to the exclusion of all other areas on the screen, you can ascertain all of the characters and the entire plot of the show.

Focusing Exercise 2:

1.  At dusk, sit on your front or back steps. Focus on a tree branch or the top of a house seen against the darkening sky. Listen to all the sounds – passing traffic, people’s voices, the breeze, animal sounds, and whatever other sounds permeate the air.
2.  Concentrate on that tree branch. One by one, filter out all the other sounds.

By now, your mind will be experiencing intruding thoughts. Let them “float away” from your mind like helium balloons. It is only the tree branch you are interested in. Every time an intrusive thought enters, ignore it and draw your attention back to the object upon which you are concentrating.              

3.  See how long you can do this exercise and keep record of the time. Every night, attempt to keep up your focus and concentration for longer and longer periods of time.
4.  If you find that you are becoming more peaceful and relaxed, that’s good. You are learning to ignore the body in favor of the mind. You are also learning the benefits of unidirectional focus.

As you become more proficient with this exercise, try to introduce a mental task immediately following, something you do often. Notice that, in time, you will be able to complete that little task in less time than it used to take you. One of the most frequently applied tests of this is to read an article in a magazine. Any article. Note how much your powers of concentration have been sharpened. Also note how little time it took you to absorb the material. That is the kind of process that will help you get tasks done at one sitting.

Some practitioners recommend the use of “binaural beats” – recordings using low frequency sounds as background music while studying, reading, or performing a task.

MINDFULNESS

“Mindfulness” is a very trendy topic nowadays. However, it is a concept that is much more complex than “trendy” implies. It is a concept first developed by Dr. Kabat-Zinn for the use of pain reduction for his patients. He promoted the use of the mindfulness technique in meditation. However, there are many different applications for mindfulness besides meditation.  Its practice has been heavily researched and tested. It is very fruitful in bringing about heightened powers of attention. Sessions of mindfulness can be done anytime. It is a focus upon the “here-and-now.” It is the single focus on a task that you are doing at the present time. The focus is complete because you are literally “slowing down in order to speed up.” You are taking in the whole experience of whatever you are doing, but – all the time – excluding distractions.

In the morning, have you ever noticed how many things you forgot to do while getting ready? You forgot your pen, your report, your sweater…your locker room keys. Then you have to stop before getting into your car, go back, locate whatever items you overlooked, and then leave again. Time wasted.

Most people race through their meals. They do not take the time to enjoy the flavors and textures of the foods they eat. It is as if they are competing to see who finishes first. The next time you have a family supper, just take a nibble or two for yourself. Then pause and watch your family. Notice how they race. Notice their table manners. Notice the various techniques they utilize in order to consume as much as they can, as fast as they can. Three days from now, no one at the table will remember what they ate. They took no time to savor their food. Most gourmet cooks in your house are often disappointed because they focused upon making the meal tasty. In fact, they may have spent well over an hour preparing it. Then it took the family less than 15 minutes to consume it. No one noticed the fact that the cook added some new spices, decorated the serving dishes with attractive garnishes.

Focusing Exercise 3:

Most people tend to think about their responses to the topics another person brings up in a conversation before the speaker is finished talking. Then, the listener loses track of the chain of thought communicated by the other. They end up only garnering the first couple of sentences the other person uttered. Try this exercise, but do not panic; do not worry about lapsing into silence after the other person is finished talking. If you do this exercise correctly, you will have plenty to contribute.

When you are engaged in a one-to-one conversation, focus all your attention upon the other person. Good eye contact is important, but try to focus upon the bridge of the nose right between the eyes. This is more comfortable for you and for your partner too. Avoid staring, of course.

Be ever present to that person and pay attention. Filter out all distractions. At the end of this, the first part of the conversation, ask the other person a question about what he or she said. Refrain from relating a similar experience that you may have had. Keep an open mind and do not jump to conclusions.

As the other speaks, try to picture what they are relating. Ascertain the total experience of the other.

Those listening skills elucidated above increase your ability to focus on an issue without distraction. It frees up your mind and opens you to more innovative ideas for dealing with a task. If you refer back to Chapter 4, you can see how two different approaches were employed to complete the same task. Why approach similar tasks with the “same ol” approach you used many, many times before? That is not only boring, but it turns people off.

How and why does this help you complete a task efficiently and quickly? Well, it will aid you in the communication skills necessary to carry out your tasks. During the course of your tasks, you will have to engage in information-gathering and communication. No task can be completed in a vacuum. All tasks require input from others. If you are fully attentive to the material you have gathered in researching a task, and if you are focused upon the responses others give you to, you will benefit. Hence, you will not need a multitude of resources to complete your job. It will take you less time.

For example, if you can get all the information you need from three websites, why jump around to eight others? If you can filter out the distracting ads, and if you can ignore the irrelevant links, then the job is done much faster.

There is always more than one project ahead of you. Different projects call for different characteristics. The same structural solution cannot be applied to a second task. David Allen said: “I have never seen any two projects that needed to have exactly the same amount of structure and detail developed in order to get things off people’s minds and moving successfully.”

Numbering and Email Communication

Do others sometimes ask you to clarify what you asked or wanted in your email to them? Do you have the same experience when someone sends an email to you? Fast-thinking people often fall into the trap of getting so caught up in their own ideas, that their communications do not convey enough information to the receiver. As result, there may be follow-up questions you need to ask, or the receiver needs to ask the questions themselves in order to understand the totality of what it is wanted. No one is really that telepathic.

It is a very helpful practice for you to number all the items you need to communicate to another in an email. That provides you and the receiver with clarity. Numbering also helps you to clarify the issue in your own mind. If your ideas are pouring in rapidly, it is easy to miss something. That is why it is crucial to “slow down in order to speed up.”

In truth, about 80 percent of people only read the first sentence of two of an email. In the beginning of your communication, politely ask the other to read and reflect on ALL your questions or items. Ask them to answer or confirm that they have received the email. Repetition represents delay.

Reading

As trite as it sounds, reading articles, novellas, and books increase one’s power to focus. Very few people read a full article on the Internet. Even fewer people manage to read one full-size book in the course of an entire year!

THE VALUE OF PEN \
AND PAPER
==================

“Paper rocks!”

-Stever Robbins

There are some companies that insist on a “clean desk” policy. That generally means that there is nothing on an employee’s desk at the end of day but a computer. Paper notes and notebooks are still important. What happens if the computer is down?

People of thought generally maintain at least four spiral ring notebooks about 8.5” X 11” high. Some they keep at home; some are in their desk drawers or lockers at work. Other people prefer only one notebook, but they have an effective system for categorizing contents. Whiteboards and easels are excellent tools because of their large size. Roll-o-dexes or similar products are terrific items for recording passwords. The computer is the worst possible place to keep short notes. Many folks create different themes for their various notepads. One is for thematic projects; another for miscellaneous thoughts and ideas; another for diagrams of task-related structures; and another for recording references.

The Calendar

Never reject the good ol’ fashioned calendar! Children come rushing in from school making an ear-deafening racket! Tucked away in their backpacks are permission slips all wrinkled up.

Moms, you know that story…as the school bus is pulling up, little Tim races over to you to sign a permission slip to let him go on a field trip or take part in a school activity. Naturally, you react in a minor panic because you realize that you would have liked to be on the parents’ committee to accompany the children. It is very important to them, but little kids lack foresight. Sometimes sports schedules “pop” up, and the children may get thrown off their teams if they miss too many practices. However, you have no crystal ball that will tell you what their schedule is unless you drag it out of them or dig through their backpack like a meddling Mom.

That’s what your calendar is for. Smart parents everywhere have them posted in their kitchens across the world. Notations of times and topics are written in the boxes. Even if your children at not top performers, they love to look up at the bleachers to see if you are there. It is a sign of love that will strengthen that bond between you and your children.

When your children enter into the “tween” years and become teenagers, it is their natural tendency not to share a lot of their feelings with you. However, it is quite true that children who see you participating in a very real way by showing up for school activities, performances, and sports events when they are younger, are much more likely to approach you for advice in dealing with their peers as they mature. This is the time when they need you the most.

The calendar bonus is for you and your spouse as well. With the trusty calendar at hand, you two can schedule time for yourselves. Perhaps you might go out together and get a babysitter in for the children. In this hectic world, many beautiful marriages grow weak because the parents do not personally grow together. The precious bond they once had fades, but often doesn’t have to. The more time a couple spends together, the more likely it is that they will develop more common interests. Marriage is “sharing a life.” It is the most important investment you can ever make.

The only drawback to paper note-taking is the fact that the pen keeps running away from the paper. Many homes have a few notepads, a couple stacks of Post-it notes, and a thousand pens and pencils…elsewhere.

The Print-Out

Sometimes you will come across something on the Internet or elsewhere that makes for a good future reference. Rather than store it on your computer, just print it out and put it in your desk.

The Post-It Phenomenon

Many desktop computers have Post-It notes stuck to the lower frames. Post-It’s all over the refrigerator. Those areas are valuable reference zones. If you want a good repairman, visit your neighbor’s refrigerator. If you are looking for a web site to use for stock art, check your co-worker’s desktop computer – just the Post-It section. Many notes are better recalled if left on notes using the faithful pen-and-paper system. This really isn’t a paperless society.

GETTING THE\
TASKs OF LIVING done 
=====================

“The home is not a

business, but it is a

management job.”

-Mystie Winckler

If you permit work to continue beyond the boundary of your normal work day, your work may expand to occupy ALL your time. You can end up being a slave to your tasks. Everyone recognizes the importance of getting tasks done during work.

However, sometimes tasks seem to require more than ordinary work

hours. Hence there are many, many people around the world who

work late. They work late one day, then the next day, and so on until

they only work. They have no life. It is very easy to let your tasks control you, rather than arranging your life so that you are in control of your tasks.

The purpose of work is to earn a living. It is to make money in order to support yourself and your family. Those prime time tasks at work revolve around an ultimate purpose. The ultimate purpose is two-fold: 1) making society productive and successful, and 2) living a happy and successful life yourself. Work is only half of the task of living. You and your family represent the other half.

Having a successful career and making money for your company is not fulfilling in and of itself. Its purpose is to keep life moving along efficiently. Production of products, services, and systems are the life-blood of business. Neither products, services, nor the systems are ends in themselves. You work to live; you do not live to work.

SCHEDULES

As Winckler has said, home is a management task. It calls for as much organizational skill as your tasks at work. However, life for yourself and your family defies the work-based models. It tends to differ from one day to the next, and one week to the next. Your work tasks are based on the daily framework. Your tasks at home are more encompassing.

You cannot set up your home like a business and treat yourself, your spouse, and your family like employees. What you can do is to divide your home life into two segments:

1.  Immutable
2.  Mutable

This set-up is similar to that you set up for your work. However, because of the nature of family and its activities, there should be a lot of flexibility within your prime time areas. Psychologists agree that there should be frequent times the whole family is together.

John DeGraaf, a filmmaker and author, has stated: “We are trapped in a work-and-spend cycle that also traps us in longer and longer work hours.” Making an immutable but flexible schedule like the one in this chapter will help you and your family appreciate the fact that your family time merits as much attention as your work time. You earn money in order to have sufficient resources for your family. But what’s the point, if there is no family around you to appreciate it? Be sure your entire family abides by an immutable but flexible schedule.

Immutable Schedule

The scheduling concept that you employ at work functions more efficiently if there are activities within your home environment that do not generally change from day to day during a 5-day week. The weekends are different. Many healthy families create a daily schedule that includes time for one’s spouse and one’s children. The children-focused schedule might include: helping your child with his or her homework, and playing with your child. You and your spouse need a time that is exclusive from the children. That might be done after the children go to bed. There is also a need for family time. The healthy approach is to spend one meal together as a family. For your immutable tasks, you can employ exactly the same technique for getting the tasks done as you do in your work environment.

The immutable schedule is based on the “sacred times” precious to the family and to yourself. Those are the “together” times. Below is an example of a tentative schedule:

HOME
5:30-6.00 PM - Help Tim with his math and other assignments. 
6.00-7.00 PM - Supper
7.00-8.30 PM - Play Time
You can split up this time for your own outside group meetings, play time with the children, exercising, reading, pursuit of a secondary activity or a side pursuit.
8:30 PM - Spouse Time

Outcomes for Work and Outcomes for Family

Projects relating to your work tasks are similar to tasks relating to your family life. Each one of your work tasks had definite objectives. For example, in one of the exercises, it stated the desired outcome: “Employee Socialization for the Purpose of Promoting Cooperative Employee Teamwork.” Similarly, your participation in a sports activity, like playing soccer with your children, is “socialization for the purpose of promoting family teamwork.” It is not as structured as your work task, but accomplishes the same objective. The reward isn’t money, but the reward is the promotion of achievement-oriented behavior by your children. You also will feel the rewards of this as a proud parent. Raising your family is your task at home, and you will want to do that as well as you perform your tasks at work.

In his study, Robert Putnam of Harvard University has discovered that:

- Children from families with little social bonding demonstrate lower educational performance, more crime, child suicide, and teen pregnancy than those who do bond.
- Studies in Scandinavia, the United States, and Japan have shown that people who are socially disconnected are much more likely to die earlier than their well-bonded counterparts.

You can be just as successful at home as you are at work, if you apply the same principles. You can also be successful pursuing a side business as well.

The Almighty Power of “No”

Do you spend your meal time servicing your work-related calls? When Alexander Graham Bell pursued his invention of the telephone, he intended that it be used only as a tool of communication, not a system of behavior control. If you think about your phone for a moment, you will realize that it always interrupts you. In reality, it has become more of an interruptive device rather than a simple tool like a wrench.

If you are self-employed, or if you have projects on the side, clients will call you. When they call, they want you to drop whatever you are doing and service their needs. The simple application of “No” is an effective way of controlling their behavior, and relegating it to a more appropriate time. During your play time with your child, why not let your phone calls go to voice messaging? Is the client’s need a matter of life or death? No. Many self-employed professionals instruct their regular clients that they only answer and/or return calls during a particular time period. You cannot help them while showering anyway! There are limits, but you have to set them.

Sometimes Aunt Bertha may say to you upon your return call (later): “I tried to reach you, but you didn’t pick up.” Aunt Bertha is trying to use guilt as a motivator for you to make her your top priority. She does not do that deliberately, and a simple statement like “I was playing basketball with Tim” will be sufficient. Remember, though, that it isn’t necessary that you answer her implied inquiry. Aunt Bertha is not in charge of your family time; you are. Likewise, the same philosophy should be applied to your clients.

It is also rude to spin through your cell phone messages while you are eating supper with the family, dining with your relatives or friends or carrying on other activities like driving.

Many marriages collapse due to the fact that people do not separate business from home duties. What husband or wife wants to be interrupted just before they engage in a little love-making? Yes, the proverbial Aunt Bertha must wait. Even young children should be taught when not to interrupt Mommy and Dad.

Some cultures require that children attend school for very extended periods of time. However, many of them, like Japan, have week-long holidays during which the parents do not work, and children do not go to school. Only hospital and emergency services are open. All other businesses shut down. In some Latin countries, there is the time-honored “Siesta.” Much to the chagrin of tourists, restaurants and cafés are not open and neither are the stores. Every human being needs time for themselves and their family. The well-known rap artist, Nas, has said: “I don’t go out unless I’m working. My quality time is when I’m doing nothing.”

TIME, SPACE, AND TOOLS FOLLOWING

YOUR WORK MODEL

The essential requirements of the work method recommended in this book are time, space, and tools. Of these, time is the most important. So, what monster has been prowling your home life and stolen your time?

There are schedules for children’s sports, which are good, of course. However, all those interests need to be limited in order to enhance your child’s mental health and reduce the stress caused by chaos. The same is true for you as parents. Perhaps some activities need to be dropped or limited. You might also have a side business that should occupy some of your home time.

The Things

As applies to work, your things are your tools. However, the piles around your house tend to multiply, and the phenomenon has given rise to books about decluttering your home. There is simply TOO MUCH STUFF in your home. There is stuff for every imaginable purpose and plenty of leftover stuff for which the uses have expired. Those are no longer tools you can use. People suggest selling your items or donating them. Of course, they overlook the fact that you don’t even have the time to collect them. The monster of stolen time is afoot again gathering things. He has convinced people that things are the necessary trappings of the good life. Plan effectively and take the time it needs to reduce the clutter. It will free up your mind.

Ingenious marketers command that you purchase their wares, or you will lose out somehow. “Last chance to save \$100,” they say. You will save \$100, if you don’t spend it by paying more than \$100. It is vital for your mental health to control that marketing input.

Space

Refer to the home schedule as seen earlier in this chapter. Some of those time segments collide with other duties you might have. Permitting that to happen will rob you and your family of the psychological space you need for bonding. Something has to give. It might be a commitment that you need to eliminate.

- It is time to make some choices. Obviously, you cannot do everything although the world wants you to. Choices like that will be difficult but essential.
- Drop some of your projects. File those away in the “Later/Someday” tray, just like you did with your work schedule. Or delete them.
- Set a priority list of activities you have to do, like shopping, errands, calling your boss, contacting Helen about her dinner invitation, and the like.

Note: There is no law or mandate that you must participate in social media. Some people do, but it is for their side business. That then, would be a priority task for them.

- Watch less TV.
- Set a specific time to check your email. Those folks will have to step aside for your personal needs and those of your family. They will survive without your immediate input.

There was a dynamic involved in getting a task done at one sitting. It is well-known in the field of organization development and psychology. It has been tested through a process called “action research.” When you set up a task, you were planning actions to make something happen. That is the ultimate purpose of the task. The processes recommended in this book are effective because they have been proven over and over again. Of course, you need to approach the task resolutely and without bias.

The diagram for the process looks like this generally:

Planning
-Preliminary work
-Data collection
-Research
-Analysis of limitations including monetary considerations
-Formulation of actions (action planning)
Actions
-Assessment of feasible techniques for goal accomplishment
-Communication with those involved in the overall system
-Securing equipment needed to accomplish the task 
Results
-Behavioral changes
-Outcomes
-Feedback loop

Does that look tedious? Well, it’s not a party. It’s work. The reward is the party.

If you can make it your business to do the preliminary planning, and formulate an action plan, you can achieve your goal in one sitting.

Many trendy pieces of writing address the human needs: proper sleep, proper diet, and exercise. You can be thin; you can be fat; you can be well rested; you can be tired; you can be physically fit or not.

But you can still get your task accomplished in just one sitting.

All it takes is a brain.

SELF-TRANSFORMATION THROUGH

TASK ACHIEVEMENT

Referring to the last sentence in the prior segment, note that your brain is not merely a sedentary tool like a screwdriver. It is capable of change. Once you have completed a major task, that accomplishment becomes more than it seems. The accomplishment has changed you and that precious brain within you. In their study of recovery from physical impairments and strokes, clinicians have discovered that the brain develops new neural (nerve-based) pathways. That is called “neuroplasticity.” Those new pathways were never there before and they were created by the brain. Therefore, an amputee can operate an artificial limb, and a person who has had a stroke can recover. Recovery and reclamation was their task, and accomplishment of that task transformed them. Similarly, your task can transform you.

The emotional reinforcement of having accomplished a task should not be overlooked. Again, that marvelous brain of yours issued out a call for the neurotransmitter, dopamine. That made you happy because dopamine can help you feel good about yourself. Self-esteem is another pleasant side effect of task accomplishment.

Even when you fail at certain tasks, that is just a learning experience. Like Edison, you now know what NOT to do next time. Perhaps those “NOT” things might even come in handy for different tasks in the future.

Many discoveries came about because of those “NOT” things. In 1978, Arno Penzias and Robert Wilson accidentally discovered the background cosmic radiation from the Big Bang. For that, they were awarded the Nobel Prize.
